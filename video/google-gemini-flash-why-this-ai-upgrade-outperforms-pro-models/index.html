Why Existing AI Models Were Holding Back Progress

The AI industry has long operated under a simple assumption: bigger models equal better performance. Google’s Gemini Pro, OpenAI’s GPT-4, and Anthropic’s Claude 3 were all built on this premise, prioritizing parameter scale over efficiency. The result? Models that were powerful but slow, expensive to run, and often overkill for everyday tasks. Users faced a frustrating trade-off: pay premium prices for Pro-tier performance or settle for slower, less capable alternatives.

This gap was especially glaring in multimodal applications—where AI must process images, videos, and text simultaneously. Most models struggled with real-world scenarios, like identifying partially obscured objects in a photo or analyzing long videos without external transcription tools. The delay of Gemini 3.5 Pro to June 2024 only underscored the urgency for a faster, more efficient solution. Enter Gemini Flash: a model designed to break the speed-accuracy-cost trilemma.
